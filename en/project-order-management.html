07.1Summary

In a business running on paper tickets, an order travels by hand from waiter to kitchen to cashier, which means delays and mistakes. DigiTab digitises that flow: the waiter takes the order on a phone, each item lands on its own kitchen station, the waiter's phone vibrates when it is ready, the cashier sees everything at once and the end-of-day report is waiting.

The app is a single desktop program installed on the cashier's computer. It needs no internet connection, no subscription and no app on the waiters' phones: phones and kitchen tablets connect through a browser over the same Wi-Fi network, and all data lives in a single SQLite file on the cashier's computer.

07.2Architecture

The Electron app on the cashier's computer is also the server: Next.js pages and Socket.io events are served from a single Node.js process. Every operation that changes data goes through a socket event on the server and is broadcast to all screens immediately.

07.5Design notes

  • Local-first: the system never needs the internet. Service continues when the connection drops and data never leaves the business's computer.
  • Trust boundary: admin screens and operations are accepted only from the cashier's computer (localhost). Prices are never taken from the client but always read from the menu, and every socket event is validated on the server.
  • Sales ledger: when a table is closed, product names, prices and the table number are copied as they are into separate tables, so past reports stay intact even if the menu or table layout changes.
  • Tickets print as HTML: instead of raw ESC/POS commands, the ticket is rendered as an 80 mm page and printed through the operating system's printer driver. This avoids code-page problems with Turkish characters and works with any receipt printer that has a driver. The preview in settings and the printed ticket come from the same component.
  • Print agent: the Electron main process connects to the server as a socket client, reports the printer list and prints tickets silently from a hidden window. The same approach works in development (separate processes) and in the installed app (single process).
